## Axis 4 — Modality as a first-class, pluggable choice

### A4-Q1 — HARDENED · _product_ · owner: Product: the keybindings initiative product owner (PM for the editor experience), who owns the v1 scope promise — with sign-off from the editor/keybindings engineering lead on the Phase-2/Phase-3 boundary AND on the Helix-proof depth (A4-Q3), since that, not editability, is the dominant effort driver (A4-Q6/Q7). The A4-Q1 'How to answer' routes the editability cut to the initiative owner; the added Helix-depth sub-call (full vs single-selection) is a joint product+eng decision.

**Decision.** Whether "per-mode keymaps are themselves scoped bindings the user can edit/override" is in scope for Axis-4 v1, or cut to a post-Phase-2 milestone so v1 ships hardcoded, non-editable per-mode keymaps. Two refinements the original brief omitted are part of this same call: (a) how deep the "Helix proof" goes (single-persistent-selection vs full multi-selection), because that — not editability — is the dominant cost driver of the engine deliverable (A4-Q6); and (b) whether the design doc commits the forward-compatible Modal:{engine}:{submode} seam SHAPE now, or states editability-OUT now and finalizes the seam against Phase-2's R3b schema at Phase-2 exit (since Axis 4 is sequenced after Phase 2 in the spine).

**Stakes.** Sets the Axis-4 v1 acceptance bar and the Phase-2/Phase-3 boundary. Putting editability in scope pulls two unrelated, deep Phase-2 refactors (Axis-2 context-scoped override schema R3b, Axis-3 binding layers/precedence R2) plus a context-aware conflict map (R6) into a Phase-3 spec that is already the highest-risk axis — and does so to deliver an editor over content that does not exist (only the Vim engine is in code; A4-Q14). Under-scoping the other way (hardcoded keymaps with NO forward-compatible seam) discards near-free forward compatibility and risks entrenching a vim-shaped, name-keyed binding path. The part that is genuinely "forced now" is narrow: the design doc must state that editability is OUT of Axis-4 v1. The part that is NOT forced now — and should not be bet — is the exact seam shape, which can be confirmed against R3b once Phase 2 lands, because the spine sequences Axis 4 after Phase 2. The larger latent stakes the original brief understated: the engine + Helix proof that Option A keeps is itself high-effort (net-new selection-set model per A4-Q6; mid-session swap semantics per A4-Q7; two duplicated VimHandler impls to collapse per A4-Q4), so the real scope lever is the Helix-proof depth (A4-Q3), not editability.
